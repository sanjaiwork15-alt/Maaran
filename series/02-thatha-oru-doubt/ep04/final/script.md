## Fact check
- The flowers on a tomato truss open over days to weeks, so fruits on one plant are of different ages; each fruit
  also matures at its own rate (position on the plant, light, temperature), so green and red fruit hang together.
  Sources: TNAU Agritech Portal, Tomato (crop production, harvest at turning/pink stage for distant markets);
  Grierson & Kader, "Fruit ripening and quality", in *The Tomato Crop* (Chapman & Hall, 1986).
- During ripening chlorophyll is broken down (green fades) and carotenoids, mainly the red pigment lycopene with
  orange beta-carotene, accumulate (red colour). Same sources; USDA tomato colour stages green → breaker → turning →
  pink → light red → red.
- Green tomatoes picked at the mature-green/breaker stage ripen at home (tomato is a climacteric fruit) – consistent
  with the comment question.

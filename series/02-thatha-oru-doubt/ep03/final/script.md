## Fact check
- Onion cells keep sulphur-containing amino-acid compounds (S-alk(en)yl-L-cysteine sulphoxides) apart from the enzyme
  alliinase, which sits in the vacuole. Cutting breaks the cells; the enzyme makes sulphenic acids, and the enzyme
  lachrymatory-factor synthase turns them into syn-propanethial-S-oxide, a volatile gas. It reaches the eyes, stimulates
  the sensory nerves of the cornea, and the tear glands release tears to wash it away.
  Sources: Imai S. et al., "An onion enzyme that makes the eyes water", Nature 419:685 (2002); Block E., *Garlic and
  Other Alliums: The Lore and the Science*, Royal Society of Chemistry (2010).
- "Saapdumbodhu azhuromaa?" – cooking/eating does not release the gas the same way (heat inactivates the enzyme), so
  the point that the cutting is what matters is correct.
- "Kannai erichal paduthura oru vaayu" (an irritating gas) – correct, simple wording.
